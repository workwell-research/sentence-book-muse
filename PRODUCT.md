## Platform
web

## Stack
Vite + React + TypeScript Web prototype, with existing Cloudflare Pages and Pages Functions scaffolding. React/Web is the active platform; the earlier Flutter discussion was exploratory. The root study desk saves independent local display and synthetic Copy records. The retained `/handwriting` typography demo has session-only input/settings; `/setup` keeps its existing local interests and topic note. This desk increment changes no authentication, secrets or hosting configuration and makes no deployment claim.

## Users and purpose
People who want to practise English about their own lives, ideas and work. A reviewed page should eventually join a growing theme binder with the learner's approval. The immediate experience is to copy one useful sentence at a time, watch the confirmed writing settle onto a measured notebook page, and keep a printable note.

## Current prototype
The root route is an overhead study desk with Pure White, Natural and Walnut themes. Six labeled horizontal pens, arranged in six vertical rows, choose Blue, Pink, Purple, Gray, Orange or Green accents. One optional timer sits beside them; hiding it preserves its space and changing theme does not create a new timer. A small open index has five live tabs: My Lessons, Topic Index, My Vocabulary, Review and Completed Notes. Their content reflects the one synthetic sample and its local state; these are not five implemented course stages or a general library/history engine.

A read-only model card shows the current sentence, actual confirmed count, optional Japanese meaning and browser speech synthesis. Exactly one native textarea sits at the current line of the portrait A5 notebook. Case, punctuation and spaces must match the source exactly. Typed letters immediately draw their own centerline paths beneath the native caret. Enter or “次の文へ” checks and saves the entry, then immediately focuses the next notebook line in one action. The eighth correct submission completes Copy. Neither animation completion nor the timer advances learning. Body ink is retained; associated markers and notes appear without a pen graphic while the next line is editable. Existing confirmed records resume with Enter without adding a duplicate entry. A five-step ordered strip above the model highlights Reading/Copying; Shadowing, Dictation, Reconstruction and Speaking are labeled as preparation, not working navigation. Vocabulary/grammar ink then becomes visible, subject to the display choices. Native review checks and an optional learner-authored English sentence of up to 80 characters can be saved.

The eight-sentence sample is newly authored demonstration content, not Meta-generated output, an imported private book or a real completed lesson. Initial progress is zero unless a valid local sample record exists. There is no automatic insertion into a production learning history. The older typography prototype remains at `/handwriting`; interest onboarding and the topic-note scaffold remain at `/setup`.

## Storage and failure behavior
Display choices and sample practice use `sentence-book-muse:desk-display:v1` and `sentence-book-muse:sample-copy:v1`. Practice includes the source fingerprint and stable sentence IDs, drafts, confirmed entries, checks, personal text and completion state. Changes preserve existing interest/topic keys; no previous key is reset or migrated. Unsupported or mismatched input remains editable. Corrupt/incompatible stored records block overwriting their original bytes. Save/quota failures retain the in-memory draft and block confirmation rather than recording fictional progress.

When saved personal writing is reopened, its old SVG ink is hidden only on the editing screen. PDF keeps the last confirmed personal sentence until a successful new confirmation. Timer duration/visibility are display preferences; the running deadline is shared across theme/visibility changes in the mounted session and is not restored after reload. Meaning visibility, selected index tab, zoom and playback speed/state are session state.

## Adopted notebook and desk direction
The owner adopted Work's blue Studygram notebook on 2026-10-27. The [notebook specification](docs/studygram-notebook-implementation-spec-2026-10-27.md), exact refill JSON/SVG and blue references 22/23 establish the physical page. The subsequent [desktop handoff](docs/desktop-study-desk.md) establishes the surrounding desk; revision 7 and the notebook-input correction supersede older card-editor clauses. Generated reference images express appearance. The app uses new separate material plates with live text and controls, not an image of an interface. Original references, imports, asset prompts and hashes retain their provenance. The separate earlier Work hand-template proposal remains unadopted.

The common page is A5 portrait, 148 × 210 mm, with 5.2 mm rules and a 1.6-row title band. English and Japanese are measured before visible ink is filtered. Short sentences leave an intervening ruled row; difficult-expression meanings are anchored to their actual occurrence and raised 1 mm within their reserved rows. Right footnotes, vocabulary, grammar and personal writing retain their roles. Memo blanking removes only gloss/footnote/correspondence ink; it preserves allocated space, columns, other content and page count. Sentence-only mode hides supplemental layers and remembers the disabled memo choice. Narrow screens scale or scroll fixed paper instead of reflowing its text.

Four English centerline faces—Readability Italic, EMS Tech, Print and Pen—combine independently with Klee One or Zen Kurenaido Japanese. Readability Italic is the owner's selected English default; Klee is the Japanese default. Native editor fonts derive from those same English centerlines and advances. Final English ink animates SVG paths; Japanese is static licensed text. Supported English typing and final ink now both use the same SVG centerlines. The native font remains for caret/selection metrics and visible composition/unsupported-text fallback. Theme changes do not substitute a different notebook font.

Screen and independent vector PDF use the same millimetre plan and canonical confirmed content. PDF excludes drafts, furniture and controls. A5/A4 select the printer sheet, not the finished notebook size: landscape A4 repeats each A5 page twice at 100%. Binding side/margin and print markers are explicit settings. Print paper is white regardless of the desk theme. English paths are not searchable or tagged text; selected Japanese TTFs are embedded fully. Physical printing and device behavior remain unverified.

## Intended broader workflow
Choose editable/skippable interests; optionally write a daily note or short post in the learner's language; choose A2/B1/B2; generate with Muse; review content and proposed filing destination; explicitly approve; then practise Copy. Interest onboarding, local note saving and the isolated sample Copy flow work. Actual generation, filing approved lessons, arbitrary lesson practice and multi-lesson history remain planned. The new `sentence_book_page_v1` contract is documented in [generation-contract.md](docs/generation-contract.md); standalone legacy lesson-schema-v2 was unavailable and is not claimed recovered.

## Constraints
New first-party implementation; the prior app remains independent. Preserve real learning data, imported books, source IDs, drafts, favorites, cards and completed history. No copied application source, private lessons, illustrations or audio. Licensed third-party font derivatives are attributed separately from first-party work. No account, sync, MCP, full five-pass course or saved speech assets. Interests guide source material; they do not create fixed binder categories. Useful ordinary posts can seed communication practice without judging their author. No SNS account connection or automatic collection. The future shelf has no fixed theme names/count and new binders require learner approval. No bundled curriculum; the synthetic sample is clearly labeled.

## Brand commitments
The Sentence Book — Learn digitally. Keep it beautifully. A carefully organized Studygram notebook sits within a personal study desk. Small consistent handwriting, fine rules, pale accents and compact annotations make the note worth keeping. The three chosen material families provide atmosphere while native controls remain legible and operable. Learning progressively produces the page; decoration never supplies learning content or completion. The owner retired the typewriter motif and key/case animations. No rankings, confetti or chat interface.

## Accessibility
Native textarea selection/caret/IME, labeled radio/checkbox/select controls, visible focus, roving keyboard index tabs, dialog semantics, current-line navigation, error/status announcements and a focusable paper scroll region. Mobile check-and-advance stays outside horizontal paper scrolling and the viewport follows the caret. Explicit fit/enlarged viewing preserves page geometry. Reduced motion immediately shows confirmed ink and disables replay/pause; it does not change Copy state. Hidden meanings/memos follow their visible accessibility state. Browser evidence does not certify assistive-technology, native keyboard or physical-device behavior.
